The problem

We ran an import/export business.
We had no real way to verify what we were sent.

Every deal begins the same way: the counterparty sends their documents — company registration, bank details, certificates, guarantees. We received them. We had no real way to verify any of it. A single export deal takes eight to twelve months to complete, and for that whole period we hold stock reserved for that buyer — capital tied up, other buyers turned away.

8–12 months a deal

The length of a single export deal, start to finish — stock reserved and capital tied up for the buyer the whole time, on the strength of documents no one checked.

Scammed more than once

The loss was never just the money on that deal. It was the months of time, and the stock we had held for someone who was never real.

No check existed

A clerk, a trader, a bank officer — anyone receiving a document is expected to judge it by eye. By the time the fraud surfaced, the year was gone.

The same blind spot, at far larger scale. A document trusted because someone had to decide quickly, not because anyone checked it — the identical failure repeats in Indian welfare schemes, public tenders, and bank onboarding, where one clerk has seconds per file and no forensic tooling. Why identity verification isn't enough

Aadhaar/DBT solved a different problem — and solved it at national scale

The Direct Benefit Transfer programme is India's flagship anti-leakage system. It works by identity deduplication — proving a beneficiary is a real, unique person and removing ghosts and duplicates. It was never designed to check whether an uploaded certificate's claims are true.

What identity dedup cannot catch

  • ~26% of scholarship applications flagged fake in the 2022–23 national cycle — 6.7+ lakh of 25.5 lakh, a document/eligibility fraud rate, not an identity-duplication one.
  • 1,000+ MP government employees are under investigation for fraudulent caste certificates, including 600+ Class I officers — most cases still open, most accused still in post.
  • Every real person in these cases already passed Aadhaar identity checks. The document, not the person, was the fraud.
This is exactly DocsGuard's target gap — the document is fabricated or altered even though the applicant is a real, unique, Aadhaar-verified person.

The solution

Upload a document. Get a defensible verdict.

  • Verdict — Authentic / Suspicious / Likely fake, with a 0–100 risk score and confidence
  • Red flags — each quoting the exact evidence on the document, ranked by severity
  • Consistency checks — PASS / FAIL / WARN cross-field and arithmetic logic tests
  • Identifier validation — Aadhaar, PAN, GSTIN, IFSC, IBAN, MRZ checksums, computed in code
  • 12-module forensic dossier — executive, forensics, typography, content, identity, security, issuer, financial, compliance, screening, predictive, verdict
  • externalChecksNeeded — what it explicitly could NOT verify, never hidden, never invented

Worked example — illustrative walkthrough, not a real case

One tampered income certificate, stage by stage

A clerk uploads a scanned income certificate. The applicant photo-edited the income figure to fall under a scheme's eligibility threshold before rescanning. Here is exactly which signal fires at which stage, and why the file model traces this back to real, documented forgery patterns for this document class. Signal types: FRAUD_TYPOLOGIES.md §1

Stage 1 · Deterministic
Two flags fire in code
  • PDF modified 41 min after creation — >60s gap flag, re-opened and re-saved post-issue
  • Producer string = a consumer photo-editing app, not the tehsil e-Gov CMS
  • ID numbers on this document pass their format checksums — no hard override triggers here
Stage 2 · Claude vision
Pixel-level tell
  • Font mismatch on the income figure vs. the template's fixed text
  • Compression seam — the edited region shows different JPEG blockiness than the surrounding scan
  • Consistency check FAIL — declared income contradicts the bank statement in the same bundle
Stage 3 · The court
Argued both ways
  • Defence: "could be a routine rescan" — Weak, no corroborating benign explanation for the font mismatch
  • Prosecution: font seam + compression seam + income/bank contradiction, three independent signals agreeing — Strong
  • Judge: decisive = the three-signal agreement; dismissed = "routine rescan" as insufficient against it
Stage 4 · Result
Verdict issued
  • LIKELY FAKE
  • risk score 82, confidence 77
  • externalChecksNeeded: confirm the certificate number against the e-District portal directly — never via a contact on the document itself

The differentiator

We made the AI argue with itself

A single AI pass either cries fraud at every compression artifact, or waves everything through. So every scan runs a three-role adversarial review, in one structured call, concurrently with the 12-module dossier.

Prosecution

Builds the strongest honest case that the document is forged — citing only evidence actually visible in the image, the ELA heatmap, or the verified metadata. Weak points are dropped, not padded.

Defence

Argues the innocent explanation first — JPEG recompression, scanner noise, a legitimate re-save — and flags exactly where the prosecution overreaches.

Judge

Weighs both against the binding code-computed facts from Stage 1. Records what was decisive and what was dismissed — and why.

Then the binding hard-failure override runs one more time, after the ruling. No prosecution case, no defence argument, no judge's narrative can undo a failed checksum or a sanctions list that failed to download — the floor is re-asserted last, in code, unconditionally. Source: api/_core.ts:776–783, api/court-fast.ts

Honest limitations

What DocsGuard cannot yet do — stated plainly

ELA is weak on flat, vector-rendered documents. ELA depends on a prior JPEG compression history to diff against. A PDF rendered to PNG, a vector-drawn certificate, or an already high-quality JPEG has little compression history for a single resave to reveal — the whole image reads as uniformly low-error and a spliced region may not stand out.
No live source-of-truth is wired up yet. No DigiLocker, e-District, UIDAI, GST/PAN registry, or MCA call happens today. Anything that needs one is listed in externalChecksNeeded, never asserted — but a well-forged, internally-consistent document can still pass as AUTHENTIC.
Confidence is self-reported, not statistically calibrated. It is the model's own stated certainty — not checked against a labeled dataset, a held-out test set, or repeated sampling. Treat it as a triage signal, not a validated probability.
PDF metadata is itself editable. Producer, creator, author, and timestamp fields can be spoofed by a forger using a metadata-editing tool. They are genuinely useful — most casual forgers don't bother cleaning them — but they are evidence, not proof.

The pilot plan

One scheme, one office, measured against manual review

Phase 1 — pilot

One high-fraud-signal scheme, one district office, 3–6 months. Runs alongside existing manual review as decision support — every verdict advisory, no auto-rejection. This is the only way to measure real precision/recall against outcomes officers already have to determine anyway.

Phase 2 — district-wide

Full document set at one office: caste/income/domicile certificates, marksheets, PDS bank statements. Begin turning externalChecksNeeded items into real PASS/FAIL via e-District/DigiLocker where a certificate carries a resolvable application number.

Phase 3 — state-wide

Roll out to all districts for the schemes validated in Phase 1–2; add the officer dashboard (queue, filter by verdict/severity, audit trail); evaluate extension to tender-document screening.
